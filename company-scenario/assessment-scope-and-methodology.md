
# Cybersecurity Assessment Scope and Methodology

**Client:** Northstar Financial Technologies  
**Engagement Type:** Simulated Cybersecurity GRC and ITGC Assessment  
**Assessment Frameworks:** ISO/IEC 27001:2022, NIST CSF 2.0, SOC 2 Trust Services Criteria  
**Assessment Status:** Planning

> Portfolio simulation. All organizational information, audit evidence, and findings are fictional unless explicitly identified as reference material.

## 1. Engagement Objectives

The assessment aims to:

1. Evaluate selected cybersecurity governance and IT controls.
2. Identify control design and operating effectiveness deficiencies.
3. Assess cybersecurity risks and document treatment recommendations.
4. Evaluate alignment with selected ISO 27001, NIST CSF 2.0, and SOC 2 criteria.
5. Review identity and access management processes.
6. Evaluate audit readiness and evidence management.
7. Provide management with a prioritized remediation roadmap.

## 2. Assessment Scope

### In-Scope Areas

| Control Domain | Assessment Activities |
|---|---|
| Governance | Review security policies, responsibilities, and oversight |
| Risk Management | Assess risk identification, evaluation, and treatment |
| Identity & Access Management | Review provisioning, termination, privileged access, and access recertification |
| Change Management | Evaluate change authorization, testing, and deployment controls |
| IT Operations | Assess backup monitoring, incident management, and operational procedures |
| Third-Party Risk | Review vendor risk assessment and monitoring procedures |
| Compliance | Evaluate selected framework mappings and audit evidence readiness |

### Out-of-Scope Areas

- Physical penetration testing
- Production exploitation or intrusive security testing
- Formal financial statement audit opinions
- Independent SOC 2 attestation
- ISO 27001 certification decisions
- Assessment of actual third-party systems

## 3. Assessment Approach

The engagement will use five phases:

**Phase A — Planning**

Define scope, stakeholders, systems, control objectives, and evidence requirements.

**Phase B — Control Assessment**

Review selected controls against defined assessment criteria and document design adequacy.

**Phase C — Evidence Testing**

Examine synthetic records, select documented samples, and perform operating effectiveness tests where sufficient evidence exists.

**Phase D — Risk Evaluation**

Document deficiencies, assess likelihood and impact, identify control owners, and recommend treatment actions.

**Phase E — Reporting**

Prepare audit findings, management recommendations, remediation tracking, and executive reporting.

## 4. Audit Testing Methodology

### Design Effectiveness

Determine whether a control, as designed, could reasonably prevent, detect, or correct the relevant risk.

### Operating Effectiveness

Determine whether a control operated as intended during the defined review period, based on available evidence.

### Testing Techniques

- Document inspection
- Evidence reconciliation
- Sample-based transaction testing
- Configuration review using synthetic exports
- Inquiry using simulated stakeholder responses
- Reperformance of selected control activities

Inquiry alone will not be treated as sufficient evidence of operating effectiveness.

## 5. Evidence Standards

Each audit working paper should record:

- Control ID and control objective
- Risk addressed
- Evidence source and reference
- Population and assessment period
- Sampling rationale and sample size
- Testing procedures
- Test results and exceptions
- Auditor conclusion
- Recommended corrective action

Synthetic evidence must be clearly labeled.

## 6. Findings Classification

Findings will be assessed using the following illustrative severity model:

| Severity | Description |
|---|---|
| Critical | Severe control deficiency with potentially significant business consequences requiring urgent attention |
| High | Material security or control weakness requiring prioritized remediation |
| Medium | Meaningful deficiency requiring a documented corrective action |
| Low | Limited weakness or improvement opportunity |

Severity will consider potential impact, likelihood, control effectiveness, and business context.

## 7. Risk Assessment Methodology

Use a qualitative five-point scale for likelihood and impact.

**Inherent Risk = Likelihood × Impact**

The resulting score ranges from 1 to 25.

Residual risk will be evaluated separately after considering existing controls and their demonstrated effectiveness.

Risk scores are prioritization aids and do not represent precise probabilities or financial loss estimates.

## 8. Reporting Deliverables

The engagement will produce:

1. Cybersecurity maturity and gap assessment
2. Risk register and risk treatment plan
3. ITGC risk-control matrix
4. Audit working papers and exception reports
5. IAM access review and automation evidence
6. Compliance readiness documentation
7. Executive assessment report
8. Prioritized remediation roadmap

## 9. Engagement Limitations

This engagement is an educational simulation and does not provide assurance over a real organization's control environment.

The assessment will distinguish between:

- Simulated observations
- Documented control deficiencies
- Assumptions
- Recommendations
- Untested or unsupported conclusions

No formal audit opinion, certification, or attestation will be issued.
